How to choose an AI consultant in Indianapolis: 8 questions to ask

A good AI partner should make things simpler, not more confusing. These questions help you tell the difference before you sign anything.

By Dustin Walker · October 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Why the choice matters

Search for AI consulting in Indianapolis and you will find a mix of local shops, national agencies with a page for every city, and software vendors who call their sales team consultants. They all promise to help you use AI. They do not all mean the same thing.

For a small or mid-sized business, the wrong fit is costly. You can spend months on workshops and strategy documents and end up with nothing your team actually uses. The questions below are the ones we would ask if we were in your seat.

1. Will you build it, or just recommend it?

This is the most important question. Some firms stop at advice. They hand you a roadmap and leave you to find someone else to build it. That handoff is where many AI projects stall.

Ask whether the firm will also set up the tools, connect them to your systems, and keep them running. If they only advise, ask who they expect to do the build and what that will cost.

2. Can we start with one small project?

A good partner should be happy to start small. Pick one process that eats up time, fix it, and measure the result. If it works, you expand. If it does not, you have learned something without betting the budget.

Be careful with open-ended monthly retainers before any value has been delivered. A fixed scope for a first project protects you and keeps everyone focused.

3. How will you handle our data?

Your customer records, financials, and employee information should not end up somewhere you did not intend. Ask:

  • Which AI tools will touch our data, and are they set to keep it out of public model training?
  • Who on your side and ours will have access?
  • Where is the data stored, and what happens to it if we stop working together?

A consultant who cannot answer these in plain English is not ready to work with sensitive information.

4. Are you tied to any vendor?

Some consultants resell a specific platform or earn a commission on what they recommend. That is not always a problem, but you should know about it. Ask directly whether they are paid by any of the tools they suggest.

5. Who will actually do the work?

The person who sells the project is not always the person who delivers it. Ask who will be on your project day to day, what their background is, and whether you can talk to them before you start.

6. What happens after launch?

AI tools and automations need care. Software updates, a vendor changes its pricing, or a process changes on your side. Ask who hosts what gets built, who fixes it when something breaks, and who owns the accounts and the code. You should own your data and your systems, even if someone else maintains them.

7. How will we know it worked?

Before any work starts, agree on what success looks like. Good measures are simple: hours saved each week, faster turnaround for customers, fewer errors, or work that no longer needs a person to touch it. If a consultant cannot tell you how they will measure results, they probably will not.

8. Will you tell us when AI is not the answer?

Sometimes the best fix is a simple automation, a cleaner spreadsheet, or a setting in software you already own. A trustworthy partner will say so, even when it means a smaller project. If every answer is a big AI build, keep looking.

A few red flags

  • Lots of buzzwords and no clear explanation of what you will get
  • Pressure to sign a long contract before a first result
  • Vague answers about data, security, or who owns what
  • No plan for support once the project is live

Does it matter if the consultant is local?

Not always. A lot of AI work can be done remotely. But a partner in the Indianapolis area can sit down with your team, see how work really moves through your business, and be there in person when it matters. It also helps to work with someone who knows the local business community and resources like Indiana's IN AI initiative.

Common questions

What is the difference between AI consulting and AI implementation?

AI consulting is the planning: finding where AI fits, what it will cost, and what the risks are. Implementation is the doing: setting up the tools, connecting them to your systems, and keeping them running. Many small businesses need both from the same partner.

How much does an AI consultant cost?

It depends on the scope. Start by asking for a fixed price on one small project. Our guide on what AI costs for a small business walks through the main cost drivers.

Do we need an AI strategy before we start?

Not a big one. A short list of your most time-consuming tasks is enough to find a good first project. The strategy can grow from real results.
