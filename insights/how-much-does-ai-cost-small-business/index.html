How much does AI cost for a small business? A plain-English guide

Price ranges online are all over the place, and most come from companies selling AI. Here is how to think about the cost for your own business instead.

By Dustin Walker · October 14, 2026 · 4 min read

The short answer

AI can cost almost nothing or it can cost a lot. What you pay depends far more on what you want it to do than on the size of your company. A team using an AI assistant for writing emails is in a completely different place than a business building a custom tool that reads every incoming order.

The good news is that most small businesses do not need to start with the expensive option. It helps to think about AI spending in four levels.

Level 1: Off-the-shelf AI tools

This is ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, and similar tools, paid per person each month. It is the cheapest way to start, and it is great for writing, summarizing, and research.

The catch is that each person uses it on their own. Nothing is connected to your systems, and results depend on who is typing. Business plans also matter here. Free personal accounts usually have weaker data protections than business versions, so check the settings before anyone pastes in customer information.

Level 2: AI inside tools you already pay for

Many platforms you may already use, such as Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, and popular CRMs, now offer AI features. Sometimes they are included and sometimes they are a paid add-on per user.

This is often the best value because your data is already there and your team already knows the software. Before you buy anything new, it is worth finding out what you are already paying for.

Level 3: Custom automations with AI

This is where AI starts doing real work on its own. For example, a system that reads incoming PDFs and fills in your CRM, or one that sorts your inbox and drafts replies for a person to approve.

Costs here usually come in two parts:

  • A one-time build. Someone designs the workflow, connects your tools, and tests it.
  • Small ongoing costs. Automation platform fees, hosting, and AI usage fees that go up and down with how much you use it.

For most small businesses, this level is where the clearest return shows up, because it removes work instead of just speeding it up. Our workflow automation page has more examples.

Level 4: Custom AI apps

A purpose-built tool, like a quoting assistant trained on your past jobs or a private assistant that answers questions from your own manuals. This is the biggest investment and should usually come after a smaller project has proven the value.

The costs most quotes leave out

The price of the software is rarely the whole picture. Plan for these too:

  • Your team's time. Someone needs to explain the process, test the results, and learn the new way of working.
  • Data cleanup. AI works best when your information is organized. Messy files and duplicate records slow everything down.
  • Security setup. Access controls, business-grade accounts, and the right privacy settings.
  • Maintenance. Tools change. Someone has to keep things running and fix what breaks.
  • Usage fees. Some AI services charge by how much you use them. Ask how costs grow if your volume doubles.

A simple way to estimate your return

You do not need a complicated model. Pick one task and answer three questions:

  • How many hours a week does this task take, across everyone who does it?
  • What does an hour of that person's time cost you, including wages and benefits?
  • How much of the task could be automated or sped up?

Multiply them together for a weekly savings estimate, then compare it to the cost of the fix. Here is an example with made-up numbers: a task that takes 10 hours a week, at $35 an hour, that is 60% automatable, is worth about $210 a week, or roughly $10,000 a year. If the fix costs less than that in its first year, it is worth a serious look. Use your own numbers, and include the soft benefits too, like faster replies to customers and fewer mistakes.

How to keep costs down

  • Start with one workflow. Prove the value before you expand.
  • Use what you already own. Check the AI features in your current software first.
  • Ask for a fixed scope. A clear price for a clear result beats an open-ended retainer.
  • Cut waste to fund it. Unused subscriptions and duplicate tools can often pay for a first project. See cost optimization.
  • Look for free local help. Indiana's IN AI initiative offers workshops and a readiness assessment.

Common questions

Is ChatGPT enough for a small business?

It is a good place to start for writing and research. It will not connect to your systems or run processes on its own. When you want AI to do work automatically, you need an automation or a custom tool.

Are there monthly costs after the build?

Usually, yes, but they are often small. Expect platform or hosting fees and AI usage fees. Ask any provider to estimate these before you start.

Is AI worth it for a business with fewer than 20 people?

Often, yes. Small teams feel time savings the most because every hour of busywork comes out of someone who has other jobs to do. Start with the task your team complains about most.
